IN THE SUPREME COURT OF INDIA
CRIMINAL APPELLATE JURISDICTION

CRIMINAL APPEAL NO. 718 OF 2021
(Arising out of SLP (Crl.) No. 3412 of 2020)

VIKRAM SINGH @ VICKY                                 ... APPELLANT
                              VERSUS
STATE OF MAHARASHTRA & ANR.                          ... RESPONDENT(S)

                               JUDGMENT

D.Y. CHANDRACHUD, J.

1. This criminal appeal directed under Article 136 of the Constitution of India arises out of the final judgment and order dated 14th August 2021 passed by the High Court of Judicature at Bombay in Criminal Revision Application No. 412 of 2019. By the impugned order, the High Court affirmed the conviction of the appellant Vikram Singh under Section 302 read with Section 34 of the Indian Penal Code, 1860, previously rendered by the Sessions Judge, Pune in Sessions Case No. 84 of 2017.

2. Hon'ble Mr. Justice D.Y. Chandrachud and Hon'ble Ms. Justice Hima Kohli heard learned Senior Counsel Mr. Kapil Sibal, appearing on behalf of the appellant, and Mr. Tushar Mehta, learned Solicitor General, assisted by Advocate D. Prakash Reddy, appearing on behalf of the respondent State of Maharashtra.

3. The prosecution case rested substantially upon the circumstantial testimony of witness PW-1 Ram Singh, whose statement was originally recorded under Section 161 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 on 22nd March 2017. Medical evidence was adduced through PW-3 Dr. Rajesh Patil from the General Hospital, Pune.

4. Mr. Kapil Sibal, learned Senior Counsel for the appellant, vehemently contended that the prosecution failed to establish a complete chain of circumstantial evidence consistent solely with the guilt of the accused. Reliance was placed upon the landmark decision of this Court in Sharad Birdhichand Sarda v. State of Maharashtra, (1984) 4 SCC 116, wherein this Court laid down the classic panchsheel principles governing circumstantial proof. It was further submitted that Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881 was wrongly invoked in ancillary proceedings concerning debt dishonour, violating the ratio in K. Bhaskaran v. Sankaran Vaidhyan Balan, (1999) 7 SCC 510.

5. Having examined the record of the case, we find that the circumstantial links relied upon by the trial court in Pune and the High Court of Judicature at Bombay are tenuous. In the absence of corroboration, it would be hazardous to sustain the conviction under Section 302 of the Indian Penal Code. Consequently, the appeal is allowed, and the appellant Vikram Singh is acquitted of all charges.
